# Study plan

Before you commit four weeks, read [what you will be able to do](README.md#what-you-will-be-able-to-do). It has the eight questions you should be able to answer at the end, and an honest note on what this does not make you.

Four weeks at about an hour a day, or three shorter paths if you came here for one thing. The rule that matters in every version: attempt before you open the solution, every time.

## Which path is yours

**You are building an agent and want to know what to build.** Exercises 01 to 05, then the deployment exercise for the platform you actually use, then 10. About two weeks. Skip the other platforms' pages until you need them.

**You run a platform and need org-wide controls.** Read [the matrix](reference/platform-matrix.md) first, then exercises 06 to 10. The thing to look for is where each platform lets you set a floor rather than a default, because that is what stops a team configuring below your baseline.

**You have to answer an auditor.** Read [the control catalogue](reference/control-catalogue.md) and [frameworks.md](reference/frameworks.md), then do exercises 04 and 10. Those two produce the evidence and the record of the decision, which is what the question is actually about.

**You want the whole thing.** Four weeks, below.

## Week 1 — What controls exist and why

**By Sunday you can** look at an agent design and say which controls it forces on you, write those as code, and explain why a filter is not a wall.

| Day | Do this | Then |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Read [the control catalogue](reference/control-catalogue.md), all ten families | Write down which three families your own project is weakest on. Keep the note. |
| 2 | Exercise 01, control inventory | Add a fourth agent to `agents.ts` describing something you actually work on, and run your rules against it |
| 3 | Exercise 02, policy as code | Add a policy of your own. A time-of-day rule is a good one, because it forces you to notice the engine sees calls, not intentions |
| 4 | Exercise 03, injection testing | Read the evasions that got past you. Add two of your own to the corpus and see whether your patterns catch them |
| 5 | Catch up, or re-run 01 from scratch without the solution | |

**Checkpoint.** Write the exfiltration triad in your own words and say which of your systems has all three. If you cannot name one, you have either a very small estate or an incomplete inventory.

---

## Week 2 — Evidence and the gate

**By Sunday you can** reconstruct a decision from a trace, and block a release automatically when safety regresses.

| Day | Do this | Then |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Exercise 04, audit trail | Tamper with a different event and confirm the chain breaks at the right place |
| 2 | Exercise 04 again, from scratch | Redaction on the way in, identities kept, is the part worth doing twice |
| 3 | Exercise 05, evaluation gate | Look closely at why version 2 is blocked despite scoring higher. That is the whole argument for case-level comparison |
| 4 | Write ten eval cases for something you work on | At least three of them safety or refusal cases |
| 5 | Exercise 10, the release pipeline | |

**Checkpoint.** Explain to someone non-technical why a version that scores better overall can still be the wrong one to ship. If you can do that, you can defend an evaluation gate in a planning meeting, which is where these usually die.

---

## Week 3 — The platforms

**By Sunday you can** say what each of the four platforms gives you, what it does not, and what you would have to build yourself.

| Day | Do this | Then |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [The matrix](reference/platform-matrix.md), then the page for the platform you use | Read its "what we could not verify" list. Knowing the edges of your knowledge is the job |
| 2 | Exercise 06 or 07 or 08, whichever matches your platform | Complete the Terraform or Bicep and pass the checks |
| 3 | The same exercise for a platform you do **not** use | This is where the comparison becomes real rather than a table |
| 4 | Exercise 09, Copilot tenant conformance | Even if you have no tenant. The four-admin-centre sprawl is the lesson |
| 5 | [frameworks.md](reference/frameworks.md) | Map your three weakest families from week 1 to NIST subcategories and the OWASP agentic list |

**Checkpoint.** For your own platform, name one control it gives you that you had not been using, and one gap you will have to fill yourself. Every one of the four has both.

---

## Week 4 — Apply it to something real

No new exercises. Take one agent that exists, or one you are about to build, and produce four artefacts.

1. **A control inventory**, generated by your version of exercise 01 from a real spec.
2. **A policy file** from exercise 02, covering the tools that agent actually has, with an approval gate on everything irreversible.
3. **Ten eval cases**, including safety and refusal, with a recorded baseline.
4. **A gate in your pipeline** that runs all three and blocks on a safety regression.

**Finished when** someone who was not in the room can read the four artefacts and tell you what the agent may do, what stops it, and who approved it.

If you also have a cloud account you are allowed to change, deploy the guardrail from the matching exercise and connect it to the pipeline. [Provider setup](reference/provider-setup.md) has the exact prerequisites, permissions and teardown for each platform.

## How to work through one exercise

| Time | Step |
|---|---|
| 5 min | Read the whole README, including the "Notice" section at the end. That part is why the exercise exists |
| 25 min | Attempt the `TODO`s with the solution closed. Being stuck is where the learning is |
| 2 min | Run it and read the failures. Each ✘ carries a hint |
| 10 min | Only now open the solution. Find the lines that differ from yours and work out why |
| 3 min | Write one sentence about what surprised you |

Keep those sentences in one file. After ten exercises that file is the thing you will actually reread.

## Pace

- **Faster:** two exercises a day finishes the whole thing in a week.
- **Slower:** one a week finishes in three months and you will remember more.
- **Reference only:** the matrix and the catalogue are about ninety minutes of reading and stand on their own.

## You have learned it when

You can look at an agent someone else built and, in ten minutes, name the two controls it is missing and the one it has that gives false assurance. Every platform has at least one control that looks stronger than it is. Finding those is the skill.
